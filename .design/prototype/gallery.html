What every layout here fixes, regardless of which you pick

  • The selected item's state is structural. An accent ring and a tick, not a ▸ glyph typed into the label. Today the prefix has to be built into the string and stripped on deselect.
  • Depth state is a chip, and flat is a warning. flat in the error colour, because a photo with no depth map renders without relief and that is the single most common disappointment.
  • The bundled demo is visually distinct — a neutral tile and a bundled chip — because it is a sample, not your photograph, and it cannot be deleted.
  • Embedded depth reads as its own state. A PNG carrying its depth map is not flat, and today the row says FLAT until an async probe corrects it.
  • The header carries the current selection, so it is knowable even when the panel covers the photo.
  • Add photo is the primary action, not Sort. Sort is a preference and lives in the bottom bar.

The one thing no layout can fix

If the panel covers the part of the photograph you are judging, the selection is invisible — and for a portrait that is often the face, which is the part you care about. Every option above is a different compromise on that single fact, which is why they trade coverage against thumbnail size. Two things help and are in all of them: the header thumbnail, and keeping the sheet low enough that the subject stays above it.

Implementation notes that constrain this

The sheet must be positioned by layout height, never translationY. A post-layout transform on a sibling of the GLSurfaceView is a documented platform limitation (b/7179570) and does not reliably repaint — that is the bug behind "operable but invisible". Resizing by height costs a layout pass per frame, which is why Option A's drag is short-damped rather than springy.

Resizing the panel below the GLSurfaceView rather than over it also changes the surface bounds, which triggers layout and therefore a correct repaint. Any of these layouts is safer than a transform, not merely a different look.
