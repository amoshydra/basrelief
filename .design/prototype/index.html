The gallery, end to end

Not a screen you travel to — a mode of the studio. Tap Gallery in the actions row of the phone above and walk the five steps below on it. Each one is live; the walkthrough only names what you are already looking at.

  1. Gallery collapses the sheetThe rail slides into the space the sheet was in. Height changes, not a transform — which is the whole of b/7179570 and the reason the sheet can be trusted at all.
  2. Tap a frame; the render behind changesSelection is immediate and reversible. Nothing is committed, and the studio is one tap behind you, so a mis-tap costs nothing.
  3. Depth and Zoom follow the photoBoth are per-item, so selecting loads that photograph's own values — a hard-won bug, once a new photo arrived wearing the previous photo's framing. Motion does not follow: it describes the device, not the image.
  4. A photo with no depth map greys its Depth slider outNot disabled-and-silent. It says why, because a slider that would do nothing is worse than one that admits it.
  5. Browse all expands to a grid76 dp frames are enough to pick a photo by, not to judge relief by. The grid is one tap away, and still over the preview. Done brings the sheet back with the new selection.
B — filmstrip · chosen
least preview covered; smallest frames

Least preview covered and one tap to switch. The cost is honest: you cannot judge relief from a frame that size, so the depth chip has to carry it and the grid is one tap away.

A — edge sheet, resizable
biggest frames; covers the lower preview

Big enough to judge, and the top of the preview stays live. Best when the subject sits in the upper half. One more gesture to learn, and resizing costs a layout pass per frame — which is why its drag is short-damped.

C — floating card
preview on all four sides; fewest photos per screen

Most elegant and the least efficient. The margins that let the preview show around the card are the same margins that cost you grid cells.

What every layout fixes
whichever one you pick

Selected is structural — an accent ring and a tick, not a marker typed into a label. Depth is a chip, and flat is a warning colour, because a photo with no map renders without relief. The bundled demo looks different, being a sample rather than your photograph. Embedded depth is its own state, not flat until an async probe corrects it. Add photo is primary; Sort lives in the bottom bar.

Slider designs, compared

The brief was “put the text and the percentage inside the slider, and stop the track eating the width”. The four ways to do it are below, drawn at roughly twice the size they appear in the phone. The trade is the same in all of them: a shorter track costs precision. At 54%, a 300 px track is ~1.6 px per percent and a 150 px track is ~3.2 px. For Depth, where five percent is visible in the relief, that is not nothing.

A is what the phone uses. The label question it raised is answered in the same row: the label takes a fixed-width column on the left, so the control stays one row tall and the saving is horizontal rather than vertical. Below about 12% the fill has no room for the number and it moves just outside, in the secondary colour — measured each frame, not a guessed breakpoint.

A — value inside the fill · in use
label in a fixed column on the left
54%54%

The number lives in the filled region, so the track can be short without losing the readout. Below roughly 20% there is no room for it and it moves outside the fill in a secondary colour — graceful, but two states to design and a colour change mid-drag.

B — value rides the thumb
compact; a bubble that follows your finger
54%

What Lightroom does. Precise and legible, and the bubble is where you are looking anyway. Costs vertical headroom for the bubble, and it is the one variant that fights the peek: the bubble would be invisible exactly when the finger is on it.

C — detented, value at rest
shortest; trades fine control for stops
02550 75100

Tiny, and stops beat a long track for finding “about right”. But Depth is a smooth 0–100 in the renderer and the mesh is continuous, so detents would be a lie about what the app can do. Fine for Motion, wrong for Depth.

D — one row, value in its own column
not used; keeps a value column open
Depth 54%

Not fully inside the slider, so: no overflow states, no second colour, nothing to get wrong. Reclaims the row above the track — which is where the height actually was — and keeps the full track, so precision is untouched. My pick, on the grounds that the thing you asked for is really less height, not fewer pixels of travel.

What changed, and why it is not taste

  • A theme that exists. Today the app has no styles at all, so its appearance is the OEM's. Every other item here depends on this one.
  • One row per slider. Label, full-width track and value on a single line. Halves the vertical budget and keeps the whole track, so precision per pixel is untouched.
  • Tabular figures. Proportional digits make a live value jitter sideways as it counts. One theme attribute.
  • Framing promoted, Pan beside Origin. Exact origin is the substance of a basrelief, and the one thing a gesture cannot express. Pan goes next to it because they are the same kind of control.
  • The sheet goes transparent while you adjust. Every slider and nudge changes the render behind the panel, so the panel gets out of the way while your finger is down.
  • One filled primary action. Setting the wallpaper is the point of the app; ComfyUI is a power-user path.
  • Diagnostics collapsed. It found a real invisible-rendering bug, so it stays — just not in the user's way.
  • 48dp targets, 16px radii, 4dp spacing. There is currently no shape language and one touch target at dp(36).
