Slider designs, compared

The brief was “put the text and the percentage inside the slider, and stop the track eating the width”. The four ways to do it are below, drawn at roughly twice the size they appear in the phone. The trade is the same in all of them: a shorter track costs precision. At 54%, a 300 px track is ~1.6 px per percent and a 150 px track is ~3.2 px. For Depth, where five percent is visible in the relief, that is not nothing.

A — value inside the fill
most compact; degrades to outside the fill
54%54%

The number lives in the filled region, so the track can be short without losing the readout. Below roughly 20% there is no room for it and it moves outside the fill in a secondary colour — graceful, but two states to design and a colour change mid-drag.

B — value rides the thumb
compact; a bubble that follows your finger
54%

What Lightroom does. Precise and legible, and the bubble is where you are looking anyway. Costs vertical headroom for the bubble, and it is the one variant that fights the peek: the bubble would be invisible exactly when the finger is on it.

C — detented, value at rest
shortest; trades fine control for stops
0255075100

Tiny, and stops beat a long track for finding “about right”. But Depth is a smooth 0–100 in the renderer and the mesh is continuous, so detents would be a lie about what the app can do. Fine for Motion, wrong for Depth.

D — one row, value in its own column
what the phone uses; full track, no states
Depth 54%

Not fully inside the slider, so: no overflow states, no second colour, nothing to get wrong. Reclaims the row above the track — which is where the height actually was — and keeps the full track, so precision is untouched. My pick, on the grounds that the thing you asked for is really less height, not fewer pixels of travel.

What changed, and why it is not taste

  • A theme that exists. Today the app has no styles at all, so its appearance is the OEM's. Every other item here depends on this one.
  • Values on the label line. Halves the vertical budget and puts the number next to the control it belongs to.
  • Tabular figures. Proportional digits make a live value jitter sideways as it counts. One theme attribute.
  • Framing promoted. Exact origin is the substance of a basrelief, and the one thing a gesture cannot express.
  • Centre ties control to preview. The app already draws an orbit marker; that marker is the origin.
  • One filled primary action. Setting the wallpaper is the point of the app; ComfyUI is a power-user path.
  • Diagnostics collapsed. It found a real invisible-rendering bug, so it stays — just not in the user's way.
  • 48dp targets, 16px radii, 4dp spacing. There is currently no shape language and one touch target at dp(36).
